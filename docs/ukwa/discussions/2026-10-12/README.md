### Topics Discussed

- Intentions of speed requirements
  - Opportunity for everyone to get 2 decent runs on medium sized slalom kit
  - Avoid requirement for lightweights to be using an 85 cm board + 8.5 m
  - ... or formula boards winning the UKWA titles simply because of their kit
- Can it be based on windspeed?
  - Tricky... how to we measure it given what we know about readings from the harbour wall
  - Didn't see an easy way to do it, without it being too complicated / onerous
- Perhaps use different speed requirements for beach and harbour courses?
  - Perhaps based on a few top riders... top 2, 3, or 4?
  - e.g. Harbour course requirement = 28 kt and beach course requirement = 30 kt
- Perhaps the speed requirements should be based on 10% of the registrations?
  - We discussed 10% of 40 riders, but afterwards I looked at the number of "all week" entries
  - e.g. 30 UKWA adults (fin) entered for "all week" in 2026, so 3 people requiring the speeds?
  - Perhaps X people can be the basis for validity... but no second criteria does risk edge cases
- Considered assessing the validity of the heat from the UKWA pro fleet riders
  - e.g. 50% of the Pro fleet need to achieve a specific speed at least twice
  - This would automatically exclude youths and slower women, but far from perfect
  - 1 or 2 individuals could potentially sabotage the heat, so we dismissed the idea
- Discussed separate heats for fin and foil
  - e.g. "Looks like foil weather, so lets have a foil heat" or vice-versa
  - Perhaps some upper wind limit for the foil?
  - Sometimes it might still make sense to run fin and foil concurrently
- Heat times
  - Considered 30 or 60 min notifications of the heat starting
  - Harbour course can be harder to get to, so perhaps allow more time to get to the course?
  - Beach course can be easier to get to, so can perhaps get away with 30 minutes notice?



### 10% of Fleet

Instead of looking at how many people finished the heat, perhaps have requirements based on the number of entrants.

This can be determined beforehand, and the speed requirement could simply be "X riders doing Y knots at least 2 times".

Some stats for UKWA fin entrants in recent years:

| UKWA - windsurf (fin)          | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
| ------------------------------ | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| Adults and youths              |  39  |  48  |  34  |  39  |
| Adults - all week, or weekdays |  33  |  43  |  32  |  37  |
| Adults - all week              |  28  |  37  |  31  |  30  |
| Pro fleet                      |  6   |  11  |  9   |  12  |

These stats were produced after the discussion, and show that circa 30 "all week" UKWA fin entrants is fairly typical.

Perhaps we can have a simple rule of "**3 riders** doing **X knots** at least **2 times**" for the various craft... fin, foil, and wing?

e.g. In 2026, 3 fin riders equates to 10% of the UKWA fin entries (all week), and 25% of the UKWA Pro fleet.



### Minimum Participants

### Fin or Foil

We discussed whether it makes sense to dedicate some UKWA sessions solely to the fin, and some to the foil.

Foil rounds can potentially be a bit shorter because it is often easier to do the runs and get back to the start.

Maximum of 2 heats per craft, per day. Need to be mindful that longer sessions may help to ensure validity.

It may also make sense to run the fin and foil concurrently on some days, depending on the conditions.



### Concerns Raised

A number of factors may cause a decent day not to be deemed valid if the rules are not quite right.

- Some people don't come on the less windy days, opting to work instead.
  - This should not result in decent conditions being wasted when 1 or 2 fast riders are absent.
- Some people work on the non-winning days of Weymouth Speed Week.
  - These days could still be good for UKWA competition though, and opportunities should be taken.
- Some people sit on small kit, and wait for the magic gust in the hopes of winning a pot or WSW.
  - If a fast rider chooses this tactic then it should not cause the UKWA heat to be deemed invalid.
- Some people might chose to sail on the foil, even though it is a fin round.
  - The dependence on just 3 riders should mitigate the risk of foilers impacting the fin results.




### Caveats

The criteria might be "**3 of the finishers** must achieve the required speed of **X knots** at least **2 times**".

This approach has the advantage of being simple, but the choice of "X" is still somewhat arbitrary.

No single value of X will work under all circumstances, so there will always be days that just miss out.

Investigations (and discussions) will be required to chose the appropriate value of X for each craft.
